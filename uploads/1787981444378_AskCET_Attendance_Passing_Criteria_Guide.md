# PART I — ATTENDANCE REQUIREMENTS

## 1. Attendance Overview

*Source: KTU B.Tech Regulations 2019, R8.1; KTU B.Tech Regulations 2024, R6.1*

Attendance is required because it is the **primary eligibility gate for sitting the End Semester Examination (ESE)** of a course — it is not merely advisory. KTU regulations state that students are **expected to achieve 100% attendance** for all courses, with limited leave permitted under specified conditions.

- **Attendance is calculated course-wise**, not as a single blended college-wide figure. Each registered course has its own attendance percentage, and eligibility for that course's ESE is determined **independently** based on that course's attendance.
- **Theory and practical/laboratory courses are each tracked separately** — a shortage in one course's attendance does not, by itself, affect a different course's eligibility. However, the **same minimum threshold (75%)** applies uniformly across theory, laboratory, and other course types, unless a specific course category is stated otherwise below.
- **How attendance affects examination eligibility:** A student who does not meet the minimum attendance threshold for a course is **not eligible to sit that course's ESE**, and is instead awarded an **FE (Failed due to Eligibility)** grade for that course, regardless of how well they might have performed academically.

---

## 2. Minimum Attendance Requirement

*Source: KTU B.Tech Regulations 2019, R8.1, R6.6; KTU B.Tech Regulations 2024, R6.1, R7.10*

| Requirement | Minimum Attendance | Applicable To | Regulation |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Baseline eligibility to sit the ESE | **75%** | Every individual registered course (theory, lab, drawing) | [Both Schemes] |
| Expected/target attendance | 100% (aspirational; leave permitted up to a limit) | All courses | [Both Schemes] |
| Maximum permissible leave | Up to **25%** of academic contact hours for a course | All courses | [Both Schemes] |
| Menstrual leave relaxation | **2%** relaxation (effective minimum: 73%) | All courses, eligible students | [2024 Scheme only] |
| PWD (Persons with Disability) relaxation | **5%** relaxation (effective minimum: 70%) | All courses, eligible PWD students | [2024 Scheme only] |
| Attendance floor for semester promotion (separate from per-course ESE eligibility) | **25% average attendance** in the current semester | Promotion to next higher semester | [2024 Scheme only] |

**Does this apply to Project, Seminar, and Internship?** The regulations reviewed do **not** specify a separate numeric attendance percentage for Project or Seminar courses (these are CIE-only evaluated, per the companion Examination Guide). For **Internship** undertaken in Semester VII/VIII under the self-study option **[2024 Scheme]**, the regulation states that *"the attendance percentage accrued during the internship period will be considered equivalent to class attendance"* — i.e., internship engagement itself substitutes for classroom attendance, rather than a separate percentage threshold being applied.

**Workshops:** Not separately distinguished in the regulations reviewed; workshops fall under the general Engineering Science / practical course attendance rules (75% minimum), like any other lab/practical component. **If your college applies a different specific rule for workshop attendance, verify with the Department — this is not separately codified in the University regulations reviewed.**

## 3. Attendance Calculation

*Source: KTU B.Tech Regulations 2019, R8.1; KTU B.Tech Regulations 2024, R6.1, R6.3*

The regulations reviewed do **not spell out a single explicit formula** in these exact terms, but describe attendance in terms of **permitted leave as a percentage of academic contact hours** for each course. The generally understood and consistent calculation, based on the regulation's own language ("total leave of absence shall not exceed 25% of the academic contact hours for a course," "75% attendance is mandatory"), is:

> **Example (illustrative, not an official formula quoted verbatim from the regulation):**
> Attendance Percentage = (Classes Attended ÷ Total Classes Conducted) × 100

This illustrative formula is consistent with the regulation's stated 75%-minimum / 25%-maximum-leave framework but is presented here as a **worked explanation**, not a verbatim regulation quote.

- **Recording:** Course faculty are responsible for maintaining attendance records for their course **[2024 Scheme, R5.4]**.
- **Publication:** Attendance figures (along with internal marks and activity points) must be **displayed on the department notice board for at least two working days** before being uploaded to the KTU portal, specifically so students can review and flag concerns **[Both Schemes]**.
- **Sanctioned/duty leave:** Duty leave (for officially sponsored sports/cultural events, up to specified caps) **is accounted for** when computing attendance for eligibility purposes **[Both Schemes]** — it is not simply excluded as an absence.
- **Cancelled classes / holidays:** The regulations do not give an explicit rule on how cancelled classes are counted; **[2024 Scheme, R3.5]** does require colleges to reschedule instructional days lost to declared holidays within the same semester, implying that officially cancelled/holiday-affected days are **not counted against students** as absences (since the class was rescheduled). Exact college-level implementation of this: **Not Available (CET-specific) — verify with your department.**
- **Practical sessions:** Counted as part of the course's own attendance record, under the same rules as theory (see Section 2); no separate percentage threshold distinct from the general 75% rule was found in the regulations reviewed.

# PART II — ATTENDANCE SHORTAGE

## 4. What Happens When Attendance Is Low?

*Source: KTU B.Tech Regulations 2019, R6.6, R8.1; KTU B.Tech Regulations 2024, R6.2, R7.12*

- **Below 75% (uncondoned):** The student is **not eligible** to sit the ESE for that course and is awarded an **FE (Failed due to Eligibility)** grade.
- **Between 60%–75% (both schemes):** The student **may** be eligible for **Principal-level condonation** (see Part III), which — if granted — restores ESE eligibility for that course.
- **Below 60%:** Condonation is generally **not available** under the standard provisions reviewed; the student receives an FE grade for that course, with no eligibility pathway described in the regulations reviewed.
- **Course/semester consequences:** An FE-graded course becomes a **backlog**, requiring re-registration and clearance at a later opportunity (see companion Examination Guide, Section 14, "Supplementary Examinations"). It also contributes a **zero grade point** to SGPA/CGPA calculations until cleared.
- **Required applications:** Attendance condonation must be formally requested — it is not automatic (Part III).
- **Department involvement:** Faculty Advisors/Senior Faculty Advisors monitor attendance and flag shortage cases; course faculty must report cases of poor attendance to the HoD monthly, specifically flagging students at risk of an FE grade **[2024 Scheme, R5.4]**.

---

## 5. Attendance Shortage Categories

*Source: KTU B.Tech Regulations 2019, R8.1; KTU B.Tech Regulations 2024, R6.1–R6.2*

| Attendance Range | Status | Eligible for Condonation? | Consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **≥75%** (or the applicable relaxed threshold — 73% for menstrual leave, 70% for PWD, 2024 Scheme) | Eligible | Not needed | Full ESE eligibility for that course |
| **60%–<75%** | Shortage | **Yes**, at Principal's discretion, on genuine grounds (e.g., medical), subject to conditions in Part III | If condoned: ESE eligibility restored. If not condoned/approved: FE grade |
| **Below 60%** | Severe shortage | **Not available** under the standard provisions reviewed | FE grade for that course; no condonation pathway identified in the regulations reviewed |

**Do not assume additional shortage bands exist** (e.g., a distinct "50–60%" tier with different treatment) — the regulations reviewed define only the two operative bands above (condonable 60–75%, and non-condonable below 60%).

# PART III — CONDONATION

## 6. Attendance Condonation

*Source: KTU B.Tech Regulations 2019, R8.1; KTU B.Tech Regulations 2024, R6.2*

**What condonation means:** A formal, discretionary exception granted by the Principal that allows a student whose attendance falls **between 60% and 75%** in a course to still be treated as eligible for that course's ESE, despite not meeting the standard 75% threshold.

**This is different from ordinary leave:** Ordinary leave is the routine absence already built into the 25%-of-contact-hours allowance that every student has, without any special approval, as long as attendance stays at or above 75%. **Condonation** is a separate, exceptional approval process invoked only when a student has already fallen **below** the 75% threshold.

| Requirement | Details |
|---|---|
| **Who can apply** | Any student whose attendance in one or more courses falls between 60% and <75% |
| **Approving authority** | The **College Principal** [Both Schemes] |
| **Maximum condonable shortfall** | Down to **60%** attendance (i.e., condonation cannot bring a student below 60% up to eligibility) |
| **Permitted grounds** | **[2019 Scheme]:** Explicitly "on medical ground." **[2024 Scheme]:** "Exceptional cases, such as medical reasons or personal emergencies." |
| **Number of semesters this can be used** | Limited to **any two semesters during the entire programme duration** [Both Schemes] — not per-course or unlimited |
| **Scope per approval** | May apply to **one or more courses** registered in that semester, at the Principal's discretion |
| **Fee** | **[2024 Scheme]:** A **prescribed condonation fee** applies, charged **per course**. Exact current amount: **TBA — verify with CET Accounts Section.** **[2019 Scheme]:** No fee specified in the regulation text reviewed. |
| **Documentation/records** | The Principal must retain **all records** supporting the condonation decision, for verification by Academic Auditors or University officials |
| **Deadline** | Not explicitly specified in the regulations reviewed — **apply as early as possible, well before the exam registration deadline; exact college-level deadline: TBA (CET-specific).** |

**Distinguishing condonation from ordinary leave, in one line:** If your attendance is still ≥75%, you don't need condonation at all — your normal permitted leave already covers you. Condonation only comes into play once you've already dropped below 75% and need a Principal-approved exception to remain examination-eligible.

## 7. Medical / Exceptional Circumstances

*Source: KTU B.Tech Regulations 2019, R8.1, R7.1; KTU B.Tech Regulations 2024, R6.2*

- **Medical reasons / hospitalization / serious illness (short-term, within-semester):** Covered under the standard **condonation** provision (Section 6) if attendance is between 60–75%. The Principal decides based on submitted evidence; exact required documents (e.g., medical certificates) are **not itemized** in the University-level regulation text reviewed — **students should check with the CET Examination Office/Principal's office for the specific documentation format required.**
- **Prolonged illness (needing extended hospitalization/rest, likely to push attendance below 60% or requiring an extended absence):** This is **not** handled through simple condonation. Instead, the regulations provide for a formal **Break of Study** — a distinct, separate mechanism (see the companion Examination Guide, Section 10) requiring submission of medical reports and a doctor's recommendation, with a fitness certificate required before rejoining. This is a **structural pause** in the student's academic timeline, not an attendance exception.
- **Family emergencies / other personal reasons:** **[2024 Scheme]** explicitly recognizes "personal emergencies" as a condonation ground, alongside medical reasons. **[2019 Scheme]** text reviewed specifies only "medical ground" for condonation, though a Break of Study for "personal reasons" is separately available (requiring the student to convince the Principal with authentic evidence).
- **Documentation:** Likely to include medical certificates, hospital records, or other authentic supporting evidence, at the Principal's discretion. **This document does not provide medical or legal advice on what qualifies as "genuine" or "authentic" — students should consult the CET Examination Office/Principal's office directly for the specific documentation their case requires.**

# PART IV — ATTENDANCE RECORD & DISPUTES

## 8. Checking Attendance

*Source: KTU B.Tech Regulations 2019, R4.4; KTU B.Tech Regulations 2024, R5.6*

- **Where to view attendance:** Attendance is displayed on the **department notice board** before being uploaded to the **KTU student portal**. (Exact CET-specific portal name/URL for day-to-day attendance viewing: **Not Available — refer to the official KTU/CET student portal you use to check your academic records.**)
- **Update frequency:** Not fixed by the regulation at a specific interval (e.g., weekly); the regulation requires attendance to be displayed on the notice board for **at least two working days before** being uploaded to the University portal, which functions as a periodic checkpoint tied to internal-exam/reporting cycles, not a continuous real-time feed.
- **Should students monitor it themselves?** **Yes — strongly implied by the regulation's structure.** The two-working-day notice-board display exists specifically so students can review and flag concerns before figures go to the University; students who wait until near the exam-eligibility deadline risk being unable to correct errors in time.
- **If attendance appears incorrect:** Raise it immediately (see Section 9) — do not wait, since the notice-board display window is short and the eligibility consequence (FE grade) is significant.

## 9. Attendance Discrepancies

*Source: KTU B.Tech Regulations 2019, R4.4; KTU B.Tech Regulations 2024, R5.6, R7.7*

**Official escalation path, as described in the regulations:**

1. **Identify the discrepancy** — compare your own record of classes attended against the displayed attendance figures on the department notice board.
2. **Check course records** — verify against the course faculty's attendance register for that specific course.
3. **Raise the concern with the course faculty and your Senior Faculty Advisor (SFA)** — the regulation specifically directs concerns about attendance (and internal marks/activity points) to be addressed **in Class Committee meetings [2024 Scheme]** or through the advisory-group structure **[2019 Scheme]**.
4. **Escalate to the HoD/Dean/Principal if unresolved** — the regulation explicitly places responsibility on the **HoD/Dean/Principal to ensure proper resolution** of genuine attendance complaints.
5. **Submit supporting evidence if required** — e.g., duty leave certificates, medical documentation, or other proof relevant to the disputed classes.

**Timing matters:** Because attendance is displayed on the notice board for only a short window before being finalized on the University portal, raising discrepancies **promptly** — ideally within that display window — is important. **[2024 Scheme]** additionally specifies that CIE-mark complaints must be raised **within two days** of publication; while this specific two-day figure is stated for CIE marks rather than attendance, it illustrates how tight these correction windows generally are — treat attendance concerns with similar urgency.

# PART V — EXAMINATION ELIGIBILITY

## 10. Attendance and Examination Eligibility

*Source: KTU B.Tech Regulations 2019, R6.6; KTU B.Tech Regulations 2024, R7.10, R7.12*

**Can a student with insufficient attendance write the ESE?**
**No — not without condonation.** A student below the 75% threshold (or the applicable relaxed threshold) for a course, and not granted condonation, is **not eligible** to sit that course's ESE and receives an **FE grade** directly — there is no scenario in the regulations reviewed where a student can sit the ESE first and have eligibility questioned afterward.

**What happens if condonation is approved?**
The student's attendance is treated as sufficient for **ESE eligibility purposes** for the specific course(s) covered by the condonation. They may then sit the ESE normally, subject to all other standard requirements.

**What happens if condonation is rejected (or the student isn't eligible to apply, e.g., below 60%)?**
The student receives an **FE grade** for that course and cannot sit the ESE for it in that session. The course becomes a backlog to be cleared at a later opportunity once re-registered (see companion Examination Guide, Section 14).

**Does attendance affect practical examinations?**
**Yes — the same 75% (or relaxed) threshold and condonation framework apply to laboratory/practical courses** as to theory courses; practical courses are not exempted from the attendance-eligibility rule.

**Does attendance affect internal assessment (CIE)?**
**Yes, directly and separately from the ESE-eligibility question.** Attendance is itself one of the graded **components** of CIE (not just a gatekeeping threshold). **[2019 Scheme, R6.4]:** Attendance carries a defined weight within CIE — **20% for Theory courses and 20% for Drawing/Practical courses** — and full attendance-component marks are awarded only if the student has **≥90% attendance** in that subject; below 90%, the attendance-component marks are reduced **proportionally**. **[2024 Scheme, R7.5]:** Attendance carries **5 marks** within the CIE component for Level 5 courses (see the course syllabus for the general CIE breakdown); for standard courses, **full CIE attendance marks require ≥85% attendance**, with a defined step-down schedule below that (80–85%: 4 marks; 75–80%: 3 marks; 70–75%: 2 marks; 60–70%: 1 mark; below 60%: 0 marks — for a course's attendance-marks component, exact total marks vary by course; refer to the course syllabus for the full CIE marks breakdown).

**In short: attendance affects a student's result in two independent ways** — (1) as a **pass/fail gate** for ESE eligibility (the FE-grade mechanism), and (2) as a **direct, graded input** into the CIE score itself, even for students who do meet the 75% eligibility threshold.

# PART VI — PASSING CRITERIA

## 11. What Does "Passing a Course" Mean?

*Source: KTU B.Tech Regulations 2019, R6.12; KTU B.Tech Regulations 2024, R7.17*

To pass a course, a student must satisfy **two separate minimum thresholds simultaneously** — meeting only one is not sufficient:

1. **Minimum 40% in the End Semester Examination (ESE) alone.**
2. **Minimum 50% in the combined CIE + ESE score.**

Both conditions must be met. **Failing either condition results in an F grade** for that course — a high CIE score cannot compensate for scoring below 40% in the ESE, and vice versa.

**[2024 Scheme, additional rule]:** For courses assessed **solely through CIE** (no ESE component — e.g., Project, Seminar), the pass minimum is **50% of CIE alone**; failing this results in an **FE grade** (not F) for that course.

| Component | Marks/Weight | Minimum Requirement | Applicable Course |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| End Semester Examination (ESE) | Course-specific (varies by syllabus) | **≥40%** of ESE marks | Theory and Lab courses with an ESE component [Both Schemes] |
| Combined CIE + ESE | 100% of course total | **≥50%** overall | Theory and Lab courses with an ESE component [Both Schemes] |
| CIE-only courses (e.g., Project, Seminar) | 100% CIE | **≥50%** of CIE | Project, Seminar, and other CIE-only-evaluated courses [2024 Scheme explicit; same principle applies under 2019 Scheme's CIE-only courses] |

**Theory vs. Practical/Laboratory courses:** The **same 40%-ESE / 50%-overall rule** applies to both — the regulations do not set a different passing threshold specifically for lab courses; the *evaluation weighting* (CIE:ESE ratio) differs by course type (see companion Examination Guide, Section 1), but the **pass criterion itself** does not.

## 13. Internal Assessment & End-Semester Examination

*Source: KTU B.Tech Regulations 2019, R6.3, R6.12, R6.14; KTU B.Tech Regulations 2024, R7.3, R7.17*

- **How CIE contributes:** CIE contributes its share of the total course marks according to the CIE:ESE ratio for that course type (e.g., 1:2 or 2:3 for theory, depending on scheme). CIE marks are **normalised/capped relative to ESE performance**: CIE marks awarded to a student **cannot exceed the ESE mark percentage by more than 30 percentage points** — e.g., if a student scores 40% in the ESE, their CIE percentage is capped/normalised so it does not exceed 70%, even if their raw internal performance would otherwise be higher. This prevents CIE alone from carrying a student who performs very poorly in the ESE.
- **How ESE contributes:** ESE contributes the remainder of the course total per the CIE:ESE ratio, and additionally carries its **own independent 40% minimum threshold** (Section 11) — this is not merely "weighted into" the total; it is a **separate gate**.
- **Are minimum marks required separately?** **Yes — this is the single most important passing-criteria fact.** A student **cannot pass purely by achieving a 50%+ combined total** if their ESE score alone is below 40%. Both the 40% ESE-only threshold and the 50% combined threshold must be independently satisfied.
- **What happens if internal (CIE) marks are low?** If CIE is low but ESE is strong enough that the **combined** total still reaches 50% and ESE itself is ≥40%, the student **passes** (assuming CIE ≥ 0 contributes correctly to the combined calculation) — CIE has no independent minimum-marks gate of its own for courses that also have an ESE component (only the CIE-only courses, per Section 11, have a direct 50%-of-CIE minimum).
- **What happens if ESE marks are low (below 40%)?** The student **fails (F grade)**, **regardless of how high their CIE marks are** — this is the critical asymmetry: CIE alone cannot rescue a sub-40% ESE performance.

# PART VII — FAILING A COURSE

## 14. What Happens If a Student Fails?

*Source: KTU B.Tech Regulations 2019, R6.13; KTU B.Tech Regulations 2024, R7.19*

- **Meaning of a failed course:** The student did not meet the passing criteria (Section 11) and received an **F** grade (or **FE**, if the failure was attendance/eligibility-related).
- **Backlog:** The failed course becomes a **backlog** — an unresolved course that must be cleared before graduation, and whose zero grade point continues to count against SGPA/CGPA until cleared.
- **Reappearance / "Supplementary" / Next opportunity:** KTU does not run a separately branded "supplementary exam" — a failed course is cleared by appearing at the **next available regular ESE session** (held twice yearly: May/June and Nov/Dec). See the companion **AskCET Examination Guide & Regulations**, Section 14, for full registration procedure and syllabus-continuity rules.
- **Effect on academic record:** The F/FE grade remains visible on the grade card **until the course is successfully cleared**, at which point **[2024 Scheme]** it is removed from the grade card. The **zero grade point is always included** in SGPA/CGPA calculations for the semester in which the course was originally registered, and continues to be included in CGPA until cleared.
- **Effect on graduation:** All courses required by the curriculum must eventually be passed (credits earned) for the degree to be awarded — an uncleared backlog blocks graduation (subject to the 2024 Scheme's narrow Credit Relaxation Policy — see Section 19).

---

## 15. Reappearing for a Failed Course

*Source: KTU B.Tech Regulations 2019, R6.13, R6.7; KTU B.Tech Regulations 2024, R7.13, R7.14, R7.19*

- **Who can reappear:** Any student with an outstanding F or FE grade for a course.
- **Registration:**
  - **F grade [Both Schemes]:** Only **exam (ESE) re-registration** is required — the course itself is **not** re-registered.
  - **FE grade:** The **course must be re-registered** in a semester when it is normally offered **[2019 Scheme]**. **[2024 Scheme]:** trailing students (past the normal 8-semester duration) may register FE courses from **any** semester the institute offers them, exempt from the usual per-semester credit cap.
- **Examination opportunities:** Twice yearly (May/June and Nov/Dec sessions); Semesters VII/VIII offer ESE in **both** sessions.
- **Applicable restrictions:** Grace marks are **not** awarded for supplementary/next-opportunity attempts **[2019 Scheme, R13.11]**. Syllabus continuity is honoured for up to **two** supplementary attempts on the original syllabus; from the **third attempt**, the revised syllabus applies **[2024 Scheme, R7.2]**.
- **Result handling:** The new attempt's marks determine the grade for that course going forward.
- **Grade improvement/replacement:** **Not applicable in the ordinary sense** — a reappearance following a **fail** is a genuine re-attempt at passing (different from "improvement," which KTU regulations do not permit for an **already-passed** course; see the companion Examination Guide, Section 15, for the explicit no-improvement rule).

# PART VIII — SEMESTER PASSING & ACADEMIC PROGRESSION

## 16. Passing a Semester

*Source: KTU B.Tech Regulations 2019, R6.8, R6.17; KTU B.Tech Regulations 2024, R7.29, R7.30*

**KTU does not require a student to pass every course in a semester before being allowed to register for the next semester.** Instead, progression is governed by two **separate and distinct** mechanisms:

1. **Course/Exam Registration Completion:** A student who does **not register** (course registration + exam registration) for **all** courses listed in the semester's curriculum is **not eligible to enrol in the next higher semester** — this is about completing the registration process for every listed course, **not** about having passed them all.
2. **Minimum Cumulative Credit Requirements** (Section 17): Certain semesters (notably 5th and 7th) require a **minimum number of cumulative credits earned** (i.e., actually passed) from earlier semesters before a student may register for that semester — this is where backlog-clearance genuinely becomes a hard gate.

**In short: it is possible to move to the next semester carrying backlog courses**, as long as (a) you have registered for all courses of the current semester, and (b) you meet the specific cumulative-credit thresholds that apply at certain semester transitions (5th and 7th semester, primarily).

## 18. Maximum Duration

*Source: KTU B.Tech Regulations 2019, R3.2; KTU B.Tech Regulations 2024, R4.1*

| Item | 2019 Scheme | 2024 Scheme |
|---|---|---|
| Normal programme duration | 4 years (8 semesters) | 4 years (8 semesters) |
| **Maximum total duration** | **6 academic years (12 semesters)** | **4 years + an additional 2-year grace period = 6 years total** |
| What happens if exceeded | Regulation text reviewed does not specify an explicit "automatic cancellation" clause for the 2019 Scheme in the section reviewed — **verify current practice with CET/KTU if this applies to your batch.** | **Registration is automatically cancelled** unless a University extension is granted; **no separate notice is sent** to the student. Even after cancellation, the student may still apply for certificates, revaluation of past results, and migration/cancellation portal activities. |
| Maximum backlog attempts | Not explicitly capped by count in the regulations reviewed — governed instead by the credit-threshold gates (Section 17) and the overall maximum-duration limit | Same — governed by credit thresholds and the maximum-duration limit, not a fixed "number of attempts" cap |

**Do not assume there is a fixed numeric cap on "number of backlogs" or "number of reappearance attempts" per course** — the regulations reviewed do not specify such a cap. The binding constraints are (a) the minimum cumulative credit requirements at the 5th/7th semester checkpoints, and (b) the overall maximum programme duration.

# PART IX — GRADUATION REQUIREMENTS

## 19. Degree Completion

*Source: KTU B.Tech Regulations 2019, R6.10; KTU B.Tech Regulations 2024, R3.2–R3.4*

**[2019 Scheme] — B.Tech Degree eligibility requires:**
1. Fulfilment of **all curriculum requirements** within the stipulated (maximum) duration.
2. Earning the **required minimum credits** specified for the branch (curriculum total: **162 credits**, per R3.6–R3.7 of the 2019 Regulation).
3. **No pending disciplinary action.**

**[2024 Scheme] — Credit requirements by degree option:**

| Degree Option | Total Credits Required |
|---|---|
| "B.Tech" (Major Discipline only) | **170 credits** (167 academic + 3 mandatory student-activity credits) |
| "B.Tech with Minor" | **185 credits** (170 + 15 for Minor coursework) |
| "B.Tech with Honours" | **185 credits** (170 + 15 for Honours coursework) |
| "B.Tech with Honours and Minor" | **200 credits** (170 + 15 Honours + 15 Minor) |

**[2024 Scheme] — Credit Relaxation Policy:** A student who has earned **at least 160 credits** (157 academic + 3 activity credits) after the normal 8-semester duration is **eligible for the B.Tech degree**, even if up to **10 credits'** worth of courses remain incomplete — **except** that **Seminar, Mini Project, and Project/Internship remain mandatory and cannot be excused** under this relaxation. Courses left incomplete under this policy are assigned a **zero grade point**, which lowers CGPA but does not, on its own, block graduation once the 160-credit floor is met.

**Mandatory components that cannot be substituted or skipped (both schemes, in principle):**
- All Professional Core / Programme Core courses
- Project work
- Seminar
- The **2 non-CGPA "Mandatory Student Activity" pass/fail credits** (100 activity points required to pass this component) **[2019 Scheme, R6.23]** / equivalent structure under the 2024 Scheme's Mandatory Student Activities category
- No pending disciplinary action

**Maximum duration:** As per Section 18 (6 years / 12 semesters under both schemes, via different structural routes).

# PART X — SPECIAL CASES

*Source: KTU B.Tech Regulations 2019 and 2024, various sections as cited earlier in this document*

- **Attendance shortage:** Handled via condonation (Part III) if between 60–75%; FE grade if below 60% or condonation is not granted.
- **Medical absence:** Short-term — condonation (60–75% band). Prolonged — Break of Study (separate mechanism; see companion Examination Guide).
- **Approved leave (duty leave for sports/cultural events):** Counted favourably when computing attendance for eligibility purposes, up to specified caps (typically up to 10% relaxation for officially sponsored national-level events, per the regulations reviewed).
- **Examination absence (after meeting eligibility):** Results in an **I grade [2019]** or **Ab grade [2024]**, with the next available ESE session treated as the student's first attempt (see companion Examination Guide, Section 10).
- **Failed courses / backlogs:** See Parts VII–VIII above.
- **Improvement attempts:** **Not permitted** for an already-passed course under **either** scheme — this is a hard rule, not a case-by-case discretion (see companion Examination Guide, Section 15).
- **Students changing regulations (scheme migration):** The 2024 Regulation explicitly contemplates "scheme migration students" as a defined category (e.g., exempted from the standard semester credit-registration cap when registering for transitory courses, **[2024 Scheme, R3.42]**) — but the **detailed process/eligibility for moving from the 2019 Scheme to the 2024 Scheme** is **not fully detailed** in the sections reviewed. **Students on the 2019 Scheme should not assume they can switch to the 2024 Scheme without explicit University approval — verify current policy with CET/KTU directly.**
- **Students admitted under older regulations:** Continue to be governed by **the regulation in force at the time of their admission** (2019 Scheme, in this case), not the newer 2024 Scheme, unless a formal migration is approved.
- **Credit transfer:** Addressed for **Inter-College Transfer** and **Migration from other Universities** as separate, distinct processes in the 2019 Regulation (not detailed in this attendance/passing-focused document — refer to the full Regulation text or a dedicated admissions/transfer document if needed).
- **Lateral-entry students:** Follow a **modified minimum-cumulative-credit table** for higher-semester registration (see the "Lateral Entry" columns in Section 17's tables); their first- and second-semester course credits (which correspond to the diploma/prior qualification they entered with) are **excluded from CGPA calculation** under both schemes.

# PART XI — PRACTICAL EXAMPLES

*All examples below are illustrative only, using round numbers to demonstrate how the rules interact. They are not official case rulings and do not represent any real student.*

### Example 1 — Sufficient Attendance
**Scenario:** A student has 82% attendance in a theory course.
**Result:** This is above the 75% minimum threshold. The student is **fully eligible** to sit the ESE for that course, with **no condonation needed**. Because attendance is also ≥90% is not met here (82% < 90% in the 2019 CIE-weighting example) — if under the 2019 Scheme, their CIE attendance-component marks would be **proportionally reduced** relative to a student with ≥90% attendance, even though ESE eligibility itself is unaffected.

### Example 2 — Attendance Shortage
**Scenario:** A student has 68% attendance in a lab course, with no medical or other documented reason.
**Result:** This falls in the **60%–<75% condonable band**. The student is **not automatically eligible** for the ESE — they must formally **apply for condonation** to the Principal. Without an approved condonation, they will receive an **FE grade** for that course.

### Example 3 — Condonation
**Scenario:** A student has 65% attendance in one course due to a documented medical condition requiring several weeks of partial absence, and this is the first time in their programme they are seeking such an exception.
**Procedure:** The student (typically via their Faculty Advisor) submits a condonation request to the Principal with supporting medical documentation. If approved, the student becomes eligible for that course's ESE. This uses **one of the student's two permitted condonation-eligible semesters** for their entire programme.

### Example 4 — Passing a Course
**Scenario:** A student scores 45% in the ESE and 60% in CIE for a theory course (illustrative numbers only).
**Calculation:** Combined score (weighted per the course's CIE:ESE ratio) needs to reach ≥50% overall, **and** the ESE score alone (45%) must be ≥40%. Here, 45% ESE clears the 40% ESE-only threshold, and if the weighted combined total also reaches ≥50%, the **student passes**. If the combined total had fallen below 50% despite ESE being above 40%, the student would still **fail**, since **both** thresholds must be met.

### Example 5 — Failing a Course
**Scenario:** A student scores 35% in the ESE and 80% in CIE for a theory course (illustrative numbers only).
**Result:** Despite a strong CIE score, the ESE score (35%) is **below the mandatory 40% ESE-only threshold**. The student receives an **F grade**, regardless of the high CIE score — CIE cannot offset a sub-40% ESE performance. This course becomes a backlog, to be cleared at the next available ESE session.

### Example 6 — Backlog and Progression
**Scenario:** A student has one backlog (F grade) from Semester 3 and is now attempting to register for Semester 5, under the 2024 Scheme.
**Analysis:** Semester 5 registration under the 2024 Scheme requires **18 cumulative credits from Semesters 1 & 2 specifically** — the Semester 3 backlog does **not**, by itself, block Semester 5 registration, as long as the S1/S2 credit threshold is independently met and the student has completed registration for all Semester 4 courses. The student **can generally progress to Semester 5** while still carrying the Semester 3 backlog, provided the specific credit gate for that transition is satisfied — but the backlog will continue to depress their CGPA and must eventually be cleared before graduation.

# PART XII — COMMON STUDENT QUESTIONS

**Attendance**
1. **What is the minimum attendance required?** 75% per course, to be eligible for that course's ESE (both schemes).
2. **How is attendance calculated?** Generally, (classes attended ÷ classes conducted) × 100, tracked per course by the course faculty — see Section 3 for the important caveat that this exact formula wording is illustrative, not a verbatim regulation quote.
3. **Does attendance apply separately to each subject?** Yes — attendance eligibility is determined course-by-course, not as one blended figure.
4. **What happens if my attendance is below the required percentage?** You may be eligible for condonation (60–75% band) or you receive an FE grade (below 60%, or if condonation is not granted).
5. **Can I attend the exam with attendance shortage?** Only if condonation is formally applied for and approved by the Principal.
6. **How do I check my attendance?** Via the department notice board (short display window) and the KTU student portal.
7. **What should I do if my attendance is recorded incorrectly?** Raise it immediately with your course faculty, then your SFA, then escalate to HoD/Principal if unresolved (Section 9).
8. **Does medical leave count toward attendance?** Documented medical absence is the primary basis for seeking condonation if it pushes you below 75% but not below 60%; duty leave for approved events is counted favourably in the attendance calculation itself.
9. **What is attendance condonation?** A Principal-approved exception restoring ESE eligibility for a student between 60–75% attendance in a course, usable in at most two semesters across the whole programme.
10. **Who approves condonation?** The College Principal.
11. **How do I apply for condonation?** Typically through your Faculty Advisor/Senior Faculty Advisor to the Principal, with supporting documentation — exact CET-specific form/process: **Not Available; check with the CET Examination Office.**

**Passing**
12. **What does it mean to pass a subject?** Scoring ≥40% in the ESE AND ≥50% in the combined CIE+ESE total (or ≥50% of CIE alone for CIE-only courses).
13. **What is the minimum mark required?** 40% in ESE and 50% overall — both independently required.
14. **Do I need to pass the internal assessment separately?** Not as a standalone numeric gate for courses with an ESE (only the combined 50% total and the 40% ESE-only threshold apply); CIE-only courses (Project/Seminar) do have their own 50%-of-CIE minimum.
15. **What happens if I fail the end-semester exam (below 40%)?** You fail the course outright (F grade), regardless of your CIE score.
16. **What happens if I fail a course?** It becomes a backlog; you must clear it at the next ESE opportunity.
17. **What is a backlog?** An unresolved course carrying an F or FE grade from a previous semester.
18. **Can I write a supplementary examination?** Yes — this is simply the next available regular ESE session (twice yearly); see the companion Examination Guide for the registration procedure.
19. **Can I move to the next semester with a backlog?** Generally yes, except at the 5th and 7th semester registration checkpoints, which require specific minimum cumulative credits from earlier semesters.
20. **How many credits do I need to graduate?** 162 total [2019 Scheme] or 170 total for the standard B.Tech [2024 Scheme] (more for Honours/Minor combinations — see Section 19).
21. **What happens if I fail a practical course?** Same rule as theory — F grade if below the 40%-ESE/50%-overall thresholds; becomes a backlog to clear at the next opportunity.
22. **Do project and seminar courses have separate passing criteria?** Yes — they are CIE-only evaluated, with a 50%-of-CIE pass minimum, rather than the 40%-ESE/50%-overall rule.

**Academic Progression**
23. **Can I continue to the next semester if I have failed subjects?** Generally yes, subject to completing registration for all current-semester courses and meeting any applicable cumulative-credit checkpoint.
24. **Can I continue to the next year with backlogs?** Yes, in general, subject to the same checkpoints noted above.
25. **Is there a maximum number of backlogs?** No fixed numeric cap was found in the regulations reviewed; progression is governed by credit thresholds and the maximum programme duration instead.
26. **What is the maximum time allowed to complete B.Tech?** 6 academic years (12 semesters) under both schemes, structured differently (flat 6-year cap under 2019; 4 years + 2-year grace under 2024).
27. **What happens if I cannot clear a backlog within the maximum duration?** [2024 Scheme] Registration is automatically cancelled unless the University grants an extension, with no separate notice sent. [2019 Scheme] Explicit automatic-cancellation language was not found in the sections reviewed — verify current practice with CET/KTU.
28. **Is there a minimum attendance requirement to move to the next semester (separate from per-course ESE eligibility)?** Yes — [2024 Scheme] at least 25% average attendance in the current semester is required for promotion, distinct from the 75% per-course ESE-eligibility rule.
29. **Can lateral-entry students use the same credit thresholds as regular students?** No — they follow a modified table (see Section 17), and their first two semesters' credits are excluded from CGPA.
30. **What is the difference between "passing a semester" and "passing every course in a semester"?** KTU does not require passing every course before progressing — only completing registration for all courses and meeting specific credit checkpoints at certain semester transitions.
31. **Does a backlog affect my CGPA even after I clear it?** While uncleared, yes — the zero grade point counts against CGPA. Once cleared, the new grade's grade point is used in future CGPA calculations going forward.
32. **Can I graduate with some credits still incomplete?** [2024 Scheme] Yes, up to 10 credits, under the Credit Relaxation Policy — except Seminar, Mini Project, and Project/Internship, which remain mandatory.
33. **Does attendance shortage in one semester affect my eligibility in a later semester?** Not directly — attendance is tracked and gated per course/per semester; however, chronic shortage could exhaust your two-semester condonation allowance for the whole programme.
34. **If I get an I/Ab grade for missing an exam, is that the same as failing?** Not quite — it signals a deferred attempt with the next opportunity treated as your first attempt, though [2024 Scheme] it converts to an F grade if you also miss that very next opportunity.
35. **Does CIE have its own separate minimum pass mark for courses that also have an ESE?** No independent numeric CIE-only gate exists for such courses — only the combined 50% total and 40% ESE-only threshold apply; only CIE-only courses (like Project/Seminar) have a direct CIE minimum.
36. **What's the difference between condonation and a Break of Study?** Condonation is a short-term attendance exception (60–75% band) that keeps you eligible for that semester's ESE; Break of Study is a longer, structural pause in your academic timeline for prolonged illness or other major life events.
37. **Are grace marks available to help me pass?** Grace marks exist under the 2019 Scheme for regular (non-supplementary) semester examinations, subject to detailed conditions in that Regulation — but they are **not awarded for supplementary/next-opportunity attempts**. (Full grace-marks mechanics are outside the scope of this attendance/passing-focused document — see the full 2019 Regulation, R13, if needed.)
38. **Can I get condonation more than twice in my programme?** No — the regulations reviewed cap condonation eligibility at **two semesters total** across the entire programme.
39. **Does a Faculty Advisor need to approve my condonation request before it reaches the Principal?** The regulations describe Faculty Advisors/SFAs as the first point of contact for forwarding student requests to higher offices, but the **final approval authority is the Principal**.
40. **If my attendance is exactly 75%, am I eligible?** Yes — 75% is stated as the minimum threshold ("75% attendance is mandatory"), meaning exactly 75% meets the requirement.
41. **If my attendance is exactly 60%, can I get condonation?** The condonable band is described as attendance "less than 75% but greater than or equal to 60%" [2024 Scheme wording] — so exactly 60% falls within the condonable range, subject to Principal approval.
42. **Do I need a minimum grade point (not just a pass grade) to graduate?** No specific separate minimum-CGPA graduation gate (beyond passing all required courses and earning the required credits) was found in the sections reviewed, beyond the classification bands (First Class, First Class with Distinction) which affect your degree classification, not your basic eligibility to graduate.
43. **What happens to my SGPA if I have an FE grade?** FE carries a zero grade point and is included in that semester's SGPA calculation, lowering it, exactly like an F grade.
44. **Is there a difference in passing requirements between Semester 8 and other semesters?** The core 40%/50% passing thresholds are the same, but [2024 Scheme] Semester 8 has stricter absence handling — you cannot defer the Semester 8 ESE, and any non-attendance there results directly in an F grade rather than an Ab grade.
45. **Can I appeal a Principal's condonation rejection?** No specific appeal mechanism for condonation decisions was found in the sections reviewed — **verify current escalation options with CET/KTU directly** if you believe a rejection was made in error.
46. **Does my attendance percentage roll over from one semester to the next?** No — attendance is calculated fresh for each course in each semester; a strong or weak attendance record in one semester does not carry forward numerically into the next.
47. **Do all courses in the curriculum carry equal weight toward the "75% attendance" rule, or does it vary by credit value?** The 75% threshold applies uniformly per course, regardless of that course's credit weight — a 1-credit course and a 4-credit course both require 75% attendance independently.
48. **If I'm granted condonation, does it also improve my CIE attendance-marks component?** Not automatically — condonation restores **ESE eligibility**; the CIE attendance-marks component (Section 10) is calculated on the **actual** attendance percentage achieved, with its own proportional step-down schedule, independent of whether condonation was granted for ESE-eligibility purposes.
49. **Can a student under the 2019 Scheme use 2024 Scheme rules, or vice versa?** No — each student is governed by the regulation in force at the time of their admission, unless a formal scheme-migration process (not fully detailed in the sections reviewed) is approved.
50. **Is a Minor/Honours course subject to the same attendance and passing rules as a regular course?** The core pass criteria (40%/50%) and CIE:ESE evaluation structure are described as following the same assessment rules (R11.7/R12.5 area, 2019 Scheme) — but Minor/Honours credits and grades are **excluded from the main B.Tech SGPA/CGPA** calculation, and a fail in a Minor/Honours course does not create a "backlog" in the same sense as a core-curriculum course (it typically ends eligibility to continue that Minor/Honours track, per the companion Examination Guide's Honours/Minor sections).
51. **What if I miss an internal (CIE) test?** [2019 Scheme, R6.4] A retest is permitted for students who could not appear for an internal test **on genuine grounds**. Exact CET-level retest scheduling: **Not Available (CET-specific).**
52. **Can I improve my CIE score after it's published?** No — [2024 Scheme, R7.8] explicitly states there is no option for students to improve their CIE marks, except in an exceptional University-initiated re-assessment for documented systemic procedural lapses (not a student-requested improvement).
53. **Does the 75% attendance rule apply during an internship (Semester 7/8, self-study option)?** [2024 Scheme] The internship period's engagement is treated as **equivalent to class attendance** for eligibility purposes — rather than a separately measured percentage.
54. **If I have attendance shortage in only one course out of six, does it affect my other five courses' ESE eligibility?** No — attendance eligibility is assessed independently, course by course.
55. **Do I lose my earned credits if I later exceed the maximum programme duration?** The regulations describe automatic registration cancellation [2024 Scheme] rather than credit forfeiture — but this document cannot confirm the precise treatment of previously earned credits in that scenario; **verify directly with CET/KTU if this situation applies to you.**
56. **Is there a separate attendance rule for Health and Wellness or Skill Enhancement courses [2024 Scheme categories]?** No separate numeric threshold for these specific course categories was found in the sections reviewed — the general 75% rule is assumed to apply as with any other registered course; **verify with your department if in doubt.**
57. **Can attendance condonation be granted retroactively, after an FE grade has already been issued?** Not described in the regulations reviewed — condonation is presented as a decision made **before** ESE eligibility is finalized, not as a correction mechanism after an FE grade is already recorded. **Verify current practice with CET if you believe your situation is exceptional.**
58. **Does "First Class" or "First Class with Distinction" affect whether I graduate, or just how my degree is labelled?** These are **classification labels only** (based on CGPA thresholds, Section 12 of the companion Examination Guide) — they do not themselves determine whether you are eligible to graduate; that depends on completing all required credits/courses.
59. **If I'm on a Break of Study, does that count against my maximum programme duration?** Break of Study is a formally sanctioned pause; how it interacts with the 6-year/12-semester ceiling is **not explicitly detailed** in the sections reviewed — **verify with CET/KTU directly**, as this can materially affect your academic planning.
60. **Who should I talk to first if I'm worried about my attendance or a potential backlog?** Your **Faculty Advisor / Senior Faculty Advisor** — they are the designated first point of contact for exactly these concerns, and are responsible for escalating to the HoD/Principal as needed.

# PART XIII — DECISION GUIDE

**Attendance below required level →**
1. **Is the student's attendance between 60% and <75%?**
   - If **yes** → the student **may be eligible for condonation**. Apply to the Principal (via Faculty Advisor/SFA) with supporting documentation, as early as possible.
   - If **below 60%** → **condonation is not available** under the standard provisions reviewed; the student will receive an **FE grade** for that course.
2. **If condonation is approved** → the student becomes **eligible to sit the ESE** for the covered course(s), as if the attendance threshold were met.
3. **If condonation is rejected, or the student was never eligible to apply** → the student receives an **FE grade** for that course; this becomes a backlog requiring re-registration and clearance at a future ESE opportunity (see companion Examination Guide, Section 14).

**Student fails a course →**
1. **Check the result** on the KTU portal/grade card — confirm whether the grade is **F** (failed on marks) or **FE** (failed on eligibility).
2. **Determine whether revaluation is applicable** — if the student believes there was a marking error (not an eligibility issue), Revaluation (see companion Examination Guide, Section 12) may be pursued within the notified window after results are published.
3. **If still failed after any revaluation attempt (or no revaluation was sought)** → the student must **register for the next permitted ESE opportunity** for that specific course (the "supplementary"/next-opportunity session).
4. **Clear the course** by meeting the standard passing criteria (≥40% ESE, ≥50% combined) at that next attempt, according to the applicable regulation scheme (2019 or 2024) governing that student.

# PART XV — GLOSSARY

- **Attendance:** The proportion of conducted classes a student has attended for a specific course, tracked independently per course.
- **Attendance Shortage:** Attendance falling below the 75% (or applicable relaxed) minimum required for ESE eligibility in a course.
- **Condonation:** A Principal-approved exception permitting a student with 60%–<75% attendance to retain ESE eligibility for a course, usable in at most two semesters across the programme.
- **Internal Assessment (CIE):** Continuous Internal Evaluation — the college-conducted component of a course's marks (tests, assignments, attendance, class work), contributing per a defined CIE:ESE ratio.
- **End-Semester Examination (ESE):** The University-conducted examination at the end of each semester, carrying its own independent 40% minimum pass threshold.
- **Pass:** Achieving a P grade or better (≥50% combined, with ≥40% ESE where applicable) for a course, earning its credits.
- **Fail (F):** Not meeting the 40%-ESE / 50%-combined thresholds (or the 50%-CIE threshold for CIE-only courses).
- **Backlog:** An unresolved F/FE-graded course from a previous semester, still pending clearance.
- **Supplementary Examination:** The colloquial term for the next-available regular ESE session a student attends to clear a backlog.
- **Revaluation:** A re-check of an answer script by a different examiner, at the student's request (see companion Examination Guide).
- **Grade Point (GP):** The numeric value (0–10) associated with each letter grade.
- **Credit:** The unit of academic weight assigned to a course, based on its contact hours.
- **SGPA:** Semester Grade Point Average — the credit-weighted average grade point for courses registered in one semester (includes F/FE/I/Ab as zero grade points).
- **CGPA:** Cumulative Grade Point Average — the credit-weighted average grade point across all semesters completed to date.
- **Academic Progression:** The process of registering for and moving through successive semesters, governed by course-registration completion and specific cumulative-credit checkpoints (not by passing every course).
- **Degree Completion:** Earning all required credits (including mandatory Project/Seminar/Activity components) with no pending disciplinary action, within the maximum permitted programme duration.
